Methodology: Data Pipeline, Model Engine & Validation

The data pipeline, model engine, and validation protocol.

1

Data Preparation

  • Source: NYC 311 Service Requests, downloaded via the Socrata API
  • Counted: Complaints matching %POTHOLE% in the descriptor field, plus 'Rough, Pitted or Cracked Roads', across Street, Highway, and Bridge Condition datasets (~918K total)
  • Time range: 200 full months, Dec 2009 through Jul 2026 — Only the incomplete current month (Aug 2026) was dropped — The March 2026 spike and April 2020 dip were kept — they are real events, not data errors
2

Model Engine: Facebook Prophet

  • What it is: A Generalized Additive Model (GAM) that splits a time series into three parts:
    y(t) = trend(t) + seasonality(t) + events(t) + noise
  • Trend: A straight line that can bend at changepoints — Bends are detected automatically, supplemented with two explicit breaks: the 2015 snowstorms and the 2020 pandemic
  • Seasonality: A smooth yearly wave (Fourier series) that captures the March peak and summer trough without overfitting
  • How it fits: Powered by Stan, a Bayesian inference engine — Full MCMC sampling (300 draws) is used so the confidence bands reflect real uncertainty — not just trend noise
3

Validation Protocol

  • Held-out test: 72.8% accurate (27.2% MAPE) on 12 unseen months that include the record March 2026 winter
  • 14-fold cross-validation: 81.3% accurate (18.7% MAPE) across every year from 2009–2025 — The model generalizes
  • 32-config search: 4 trend types × 8 changepoint strategies were tested; the winner was selected by lowest CV error — no cherry-picking
4

Key Terms

  • MAPE = Mean Absolute Percentage Error — How far off predictions are, on average, as a percentage. 18.7% MAPE = off by 18.7% — Lower is better
  • Changepoint: A date where the trend line is allowed to bend — Some are automatic; the 2015 and 2020 ones were set by hand
  • Regressors: Extra on/off flags for known event windows (e.g., snowstorm_2015 = 1 for Jan–Mar 2015) — They let the model handle big shocks without warping the trend

Next Steps

Prophet is a strong baseline, but pothole formation is driven by weather. Better inputs, better models, and broader scope are the natural next steps.

1

New Data Inputs

  • Weather: Snowfall, rainfall, and freeze-thaw cycles are the direct physical drivers of pothole formation — Integrating NOAA daily temperature, precipitation, and snow-depth data as regressors would let the forecast respond to conditions in real time, rather than relying on seasonal averages
  • Cave-in complaints: Cave-ins share the same root cause as potholes — freeze-thaw cycles weaken pavement from below — The physics does not distinguish between complaint categories — ~168K cave-in records exist in the 311 dataset — Though handled by a different department, including them would give a more complete picture of freeze-thaw damage across NYC infrastructure
2

Alternative Model Architectures

  • Gradient-boosted trees (XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost) can capture non-linear interactions between weather and complaint history that an additive GAM cannot express
  • Foundation forecasters (AWS Chronos, Google TimesFM) treat time-series prediction as a language-modeling problem — Zero-shot — no per-series training needed
  • Reinforcement learning could optimize the sequential crew-allocation and resource-deployment problem week-to-week under evolving forecast uncertainty
  • Diffusion models are emerging for extreme-event prediction: they model complex, multi-modal uncertainty distributions, making them well-suited for rare high-variance shocks like the March 2026 spike
